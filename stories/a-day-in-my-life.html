A Day in My Life: Grad Student, Creator, Business Owner

Sep 13, 2026

People ask what my days actually look like, balancing a master's program, a content business, and client work. Here is a typical weekday in Scottsdale.

The morning starts early and quiet. I check overnight messages from brand contacts, scan my calendar, and plan the day's content around it. If there is filming to do, mornings have the best light, so the camera comes out before the laptop does.

Midday is school. This fall I am in courses like CIS 507 and MGT 502, and the work is real: readings, projects, discussion posts. I treat it like a job, with set hours, because it is one. The surprise benefit is how often class connects directly to my work. A lecture on AI strategy on Tuesday becomes a smarter content plan on Wednesday.

Afternoons belong to the business. That means editing, posting, answering pitches, and sending new ones. Outreach is a daily habit, not a campaign. A few thoughtful emails every day compound into partnerships months later. I also keep up with the numbers: what performed, what flopped, and what to try next.

Evenings are for reset. Dinner, time offline, and planning tomorrow. I learned the hard way that a creator who never stops creating burns out fast. Rest is part of the schedule, not the reward for finishing it.

No two days match this exactly. Travel weeks blow it up completely, in the best way. But the structure holds: learn in the morning, build in the afternoon, rest at night. It is not glamorous. It just works.
