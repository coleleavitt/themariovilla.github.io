Why I Went Back to School at 30

Sep 13, 2026

Most people go to grad school in their twenties. I went in my thirties, with a business to run and rent to pay. Here is why it was the right call.

I have spent more than six years working in digital marketing. I have run paid campaigns, grown audiences, reported on KPIs, and learned what actually moves people online. By every normal measure, I did not need another degree. But marketing is changing faster than any playbook can keep up with, and the change has a name: artificial intelligence.

Every part of my work now touches AI. The tools I use to edit, to plan content, to analyze performance, to reach the right audience. I could keep learning it piece by piece on my own, the way I learned everything else in this industry. Or I could study it properly, at one of the best business schools in the country, and come out the other side able to lead with it instead of chasing it.

So I enrolled at Arizona State University in the W. P. Carey School of Business, in the M.S. in AI in Business program. It is built for exactly this moment: not computer science theory, but how AI creates value inside real companies. That is the work I already do. Now I am learning the why behind it.

People ask how I balance it all. The honest answer is that I do not always. Some weeks the coursework wins and content slows down. Some weeks a brand trip takes over and I am writing discussion posts from a hotel lobby. What makes it work is that the two worlds feed each other. A class on data driven strategy makes my content sharper. A week of creating content gives me real examples to bring into class discussions.

Going back to school at 30 taught me something I did not expect. Learning does not get harder with age. It gets more useful. Every lecture lands on top of years of real experience, so nothing stays abstract for long. I am not studying for a future career. I am studying for the one I already have.
