What Six Years in B2B Marketing Taught Me About Building an Audience

Sep 13, 2026

I did not start as a creator. I started as the person behind the curtain, running someone else's accounts.

From 2018 to 2024 I worked as a digital marketing specialist at Quisitive, a B2B technology company. My job was the unglamorous engine room of marketing: paid campaigns on Google and Bing, organic social through Buffer, A/B testing, KPI reporting, account based marketing. Nobody was handing me a ring light. I was in spreadsheets.

But something useful happened there. I grew the company's LinkedIn audience by 63 percent and increased content output by 288 percent. Those numbers taught me the two lessons that now run my entire creator business.

Lesson one: consistency beats inspiration. The content that grew that LinkedIn page was not a viral hit. It was showing up every week with something useful, long after the novelty wore off. My Instagram works the same way. People think 70,000 followers comes from one lucky video. It comes from posting for years.

Lesson two: know exactly who you are talking to. In B2B, every campaign starts with the audience: their job titles, their problems, what keeps them up at night. Creators skip this step and then wonder why nobody watches. I plan my content the same way I planned campaigns. Who is this for, and what will they get from the next fifteen seconds.

The third lesson came later, when I went freelance: report honestly. In marketing, inflated numbers get found out fast. I carry that into my creator work. My media kit leads with real stats, even when they are not the flashiest numbers in the room. Brands can tell the difference, and the ones worth working with respect it.

People are surprised that a B2B marketer became a lifestyle creator. I am not. It is the same job. Understand people, show up for them, and measure what matters.
